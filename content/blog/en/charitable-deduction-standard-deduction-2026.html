In recent years (2022–2025), only taxpayers who itemized could deduct charitable gifts. Most people take the standard deduction, so their church offerings and donations brought no federal tax benefit. Starting in 2026, taxpayers who take the standard deduction can deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts, or $2,000 for married couples filing jointly. Gifts made in 2026 count on the return you file in spring 2027.

IRS Announcement

“Beginning with tax year 2026, individuals who do not itemize may deduct up to $1,000 of cash contributions made to eligible charitable organizations ($2,000 for married taxpayers filing jointly).”IRS, Working Families Tax Cuts (Section 70424) · Read the original

Key Points

  • Who: Individual taxpayers who take the standard deduction instead of itemizing
  • How much: Up to $1,000 of cash gifts, or up to $2,000 for married couples filing jointly
  • When: Starting with tax year 2026. Gifts made between January 1 and December 31, 2026 count on the return you file in 2027.
  • Which gifts: Cash gifts to charitable organizations recognized by the IRS, including churches and other religious organizations. Gifts by check, card, or bank transfer also count as cash gifts.
  • What does not count: The IRS says gifts to donor-advised funds and supporting organizations do not qualify. Donated goods, such as clothing or furniture, are not covered by this deduction. Money given directly to individuals, political groups, or foreign organizations (with limited exceptions) has never been deductible.
  • Why it matters: The IRS calls this a "significant change," because taxpayers who did not itemize previously received no federal tax benefit for their cash gifts.

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • If you give regularly to your church or a charity and take the standard deduction, this likely applies to you. That is especially true for households with a small mortgage or none.
  • Records are essential. For any single gift of $250 or more, you need a written acknowledgment from the organization. For smaller gifts, keep a bank record or the organization's receipt. Asking your church or charity for a year-end giving statement in January makes filing easy.
  • To count for 2026, the gift must be made by December 31. Gifts charged to a credit card count on the date of the charge.
  • If you give to organizations based outside the U.S., overseas relief groups, or alumni associations, first confirm that the organization qualifies under U.S. tax law. You can check with the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search below (a church may qualify even if it is not listed).
  • If you itemize because of large mortgage interest or property taxes, you deduct gifts as an itemized deduction as before, not under this rule. Starting in 2026, itemizers can deduct only the portion of gifts above 0.5% of income, so we will compare both options when we prepare your return.
  • This is a federal deduction. California generally does not follow the 2025 federal tax law changes, so this deduction does not appear to apply on your California return.
