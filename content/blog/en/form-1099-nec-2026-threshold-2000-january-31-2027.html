If your business paid independent contractors or freelancers, you may need to file Form 1099-NEC early next year. According to the IRS, for tax years beginning after 2025 the minimum reporting threshold for certain payments increased to $2,000. This summary follows the Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (revised 12/2026) and Publication 1099 (2026).

IRS Announcement

“Report on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC only when payments are made in the course of your trade or business.”IRS, Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (12/2026) · Read the original

Key Points

  • Threshold: The IRS says the threshold was previously $600, and that for tax years beginning after 2025 the minimum threshold for certain payments increased to $2,000. The Instructions say it may be adjusted for inflation beginning in calendar year 2027.
  • $2,000 does not apply to every item: For example, the threshold is $10 for royalties, $600 for gross proceeds paid to an attorney and $5,000 for direct sales for resale, and other items have different thresholds too. Also, if you withheld under the backup withholding rules, you must file regardless of the amount of the payment.
  • Card and third-party network payments: Payments made with a credit card or payment card and certain other types of payments, including third-party network transactions, must be reported on Form 1099-K by the payment settlement entity and are not subject to reporting on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC.
  • Business payments only: The instructions say to report on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC only when payments are made in the course of your trade or business, and that personal payments are not reportable.
  • Payments to corporations: Generally, payments to a corporation (including an LLC treated as a C or S corporation) are not reportable. There are exceptions, such as payments for legal services and medical and health care payments.
  • Form 1099-NEC due date: The instructions say Form 1099-NEC must be filed with the IRS on or before January 31, and that the payee statement is also due by January 31. If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day.
  • Form 1099-MISC due dates: Payee statements are generally due January 31, but February 15 if amounts are reported in box 8 (substitute payments in lieu of dividends or interest) or box 10 (gross proceeds paid to an attorney). Filing with the IRS is due February 28 on paper or March 31 if filing electronically.
  • E-filing: If you are required to file 10 or more information returns during the year, you must e-file. The IRS says the 10-or-more requirement does not apply separately to each type of form. A hardship waiver is an exception.
  • Taxpayer identification numbers: A payee who does not furnish a TIN may be subject to backup withholding. Part N of Publication 1099 lists nonemployee compensation among payments that may be subject to backup withholding at a 24% rate. There are exceptions, such as exempt payees.

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • January 31, 2027 and February 28, 2027 both fall on a Sunday. Applying the weekend rule, the January 31 due date could move to Monday, February 1, and the February 28 due date to Monday, March 1. This is my own calculation, not stated in the IRS guidance, so please confirm the exact dates in the IRS guidance for that year before filing.
  • This is my own opinion, not IRS guidance: before year-end, total what you paid each vendor or freelancer this year, and request a Form W-9 (the form used to request and certify a taxpayer identification number) from anyone you have not yet collected one from.
  • Which threshold applies to a given payment, and whether it is actually reportable, can depend on the type of payment, the kind of payee, how it was paid and whether it was a business payment, so it needs to be checked individually.
