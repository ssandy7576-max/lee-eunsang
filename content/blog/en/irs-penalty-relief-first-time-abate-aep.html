Penalties and interest are added to tax that is filed or paid late. For taxpayers with a history of timely compliance, the IRS offers First Time Abate (FTA), and it describes a new Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP) starting summer 2026. This summary follows the IRS page, which showed a last reviewed or updated date of November 16, 2026.

IRS Announcement

“FTA has been the most common administrative relief for individuals and businesses with a history of timely compliance.”IRS, Administrative penalty relief · Read the original

Key Points

  • FTA eligibility: The same return type as the original return was timely filed for the prior three years (or 12 consecutive quarters). In that period, either no penalty (except the estimated tax penalty) was assessed, or a penalty was assessed but later abated for reasonable cause or IRS error.
  • Extra conditions for businesses: The IRS did not waive the failure-to-deposit penalty four or more times during the prior three years (or 12 consecutive quarters), and the failure-to-deposit penalty was not charged for EFTPS avoidance.
  • Penalties covered (regardless of amount): failure to file, failure to pay, and failure to deposit.
  • Not covered: returns filed once or infrequently (event-based filing requirements), the Daily Delinquency Penalty, and information reporting dependent on another filing.
  • How to request: Call the number on your notice or letter, or send a written statement or Form 843 to the address in the Form 843 instructions. You do not need to name FTA or submit supporting documents; the IRS reviews your account to see if you qualify.
  • Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP): It starts summer 2026 and applies to tax year 2025 returns and later, and to 2026 quarterly returns and later. If you filed returns and paid tax on time for the prior three years (or 12 consecutive quarters for quarterly filers), you are not assessed failure-to-file, failure-to-pay or failure-to-deposit penalties when you file or pay late. It is applied when your original return completes processing, and you do not need to respond or contact the IRS. Eligible returns: Forms 1040, 1065 and 1120; Forms 940, 941, 943, 944 and 945; and Form CT-1.
  • FTA versus AEP: FTA is not automatic; you contact the IRS, and the penalty is assessed first and later removed. The failure-to-pay penalty may keep accruing until the tax is paid in full. AEP is automatic, and the penalty is not assessed. FTA applies to eligible tax year 2025 and 2026 quarterly returns that AEP does not cover, plus all prior years and periods.
  • If you do not qualify: You may request penalty relief based on reasonable cause, and the IRS will notify you of its decision.
  • Interest: The IRS assesses interest on penalties. It automatically reduces or removes the interest related to a penalty if any of your penalties are reduced or removed. The IRS says interest on penalties increases your balance until you pay in full.

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • If you received a penalty notice, first confirm which penalty it is and which year and return it relates to. FTA covers failure-to-file, failure-to-pay and failure-to-deposit penalties, and the prior three-year history is the key test. Whether it applies to you depends on your account records, so it needs to be checked individually.
  • The IRS says it reviews your account when you request FTA, but the IRS decides whether to grant it. Relief cannot be promised, and results differ from case to case.
  • This is my own opinion, not IRS guidance: if you still owe tax and not only a penalty, consider reviewing a payment method, such as a payment plan, together with the relief request, because interest keeps accruing until the balance is paid in full.
  • The IRS says it sends a notice when AEP is applied. If you received a penalty notice and believe you had timely compliance for the prior three years, please talk to us before the deadline shown on your notice.
  • Scams impersonating the IRS by text, email and phone exist. Confirm that a notice is real through IRS.gov or your IRS online account.
  • California penalties are assessed separately by the FTB. This IRS page does not cover California, so state penalties need to be checked individually.
