On November 12, 2026 the IRS announced the 2027 retirement plan contribution limits. The limit for 401(k)-type workplace plans goes up, while the IRA limit stays at $7,500. These amounts apply to 2027 contributions, so use them as a guide when you review the rest of this year and next year's payroll settings.

IRS Announcement

“The annual contribution limit for employees who participate in 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 plans, and the federal government's Thrift Savings Plan is increased to $25,500, up from $24,500 for 2026.”IRS, IR-2026-136 (Nov. 12, 2026) · Read the original

Key Points

  • 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 and Thrift Savings Plan employee limit: $25,500 (up from $24,500 in 2026).
  • Catch-up contribution limit for employees aged 50 and over: $8,500 (up from $8,000). The higher catch-up limit for ages 60 to 63 is $11,750.
  • IRA contribution limit: remains $7,500. The IRA catch-up limit for individuals aged 50 and over remains $1,100.
  • SIMPLE retirement account limit: $17,500 (up from $17,000).
  • Traditional IRA deduction phase-out, covered by a workplace plan: $83,000 to $93,000 for single taxpayers (up from $81,000 to $91,000). For married couples filing jointly where the spouse making the IRA contribution is covered by a workplace plan, $133,000 to $153,000 (up from $129,000 to $149,000).
  • Roth IRA income phase-out: $158,000 to $173,000 for singles and heads of household (up from $153,000 to $168,000), and $250,000 to $260,000 for married couples filing jointly (up from $242,000 to $252,000).

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • These limits apply to 2027 contributions. Your 2026 401(k) contributions follow this year's limit, so do not mix the two. How much more you can still contribute from your remaining 2026 paychecks depends on your plan's rules and what you have already contributed, so it needs to be checked individually.
  • A higher limit does not mean that contributing more is better for everyone. It depends on your cash flow, your current and expected retirement tax rates, and whether your plan has an employer match. This is my own opinion, not IRS guidance: I suggest deciding a contribution rate that fits your situation before focusing on the limit amount.
  • Whether you can deduct a traditional IRA contribution or contribute to a Roth IRA depends on your income range. The range that applies depends on your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) and on whether you are covered by a workplace plan, so it needs to be checked individually.
  • This news release (IR-2026-136) does not list limits for plans such as a SEP or a Solo 401(k) for self-employed people and small business owners. The plans available and their limits depend on your business structure, so an individual consultation is needed.
  • California state tax treatment can differ from federal. This IRS announcement does not cover California, so the state tax effect needs to be checked individually.
