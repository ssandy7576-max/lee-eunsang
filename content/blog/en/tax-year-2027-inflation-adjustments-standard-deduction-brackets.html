On November 5, 2026 the IRS announced its tax year 2027 inflation adjustments. The standard deduction, tax brackets, Child Tax Credit and other amounts change. These amounts apply to 2027 income, not to your 2026 return, so use them as a guide when you review next year's withholding and estimated payments.

IRS Announcement

“The tax year 2027 adjustments described below generally apply to tax returns filed in 2028.”IRS, IR-2026-133 (Nov. 5, 2026) · Read the original

Key Points

  • Standard deduction: $33,200 for married filing jointly (up $1,000 from 2026), $16,600 for single and married filing separately, and $24,925 for head of household.
  • Tax brackets (single / married filing jointly): 10% up to $12,800 / $25,600; 12% over $12,800 / $25,600; 22% over $52,025 / $104,050; 24% over $109,125 / $218,250; 32% over $208,325 / $416,650; 35% over $264,550 / $529,100; 37% over $661,375 / $793,650. The release does not list brackets for head of household or married filing separately.
  • Child Tax Credit: Maximum $2,300 (up $100), with a refundable portion of $1,800 (up $100).
  • Gift tax: The annual exclusion is $20,000 per recipient (up from $19,000), and $201,000 for gifts to a non-citizen spouse (up from $194,000).
  • Estate basic exclusion: $15,480,000 for decedents dying in 2027 (up from $15,000,000).
  • Health FSA: Salary reduction limit of $3,500 (up $100) and carryover maximum of $700 (up $20). Qualified transportation fringe and qualified parking are $350 per month (up $10).
  • Business Section 179: Limit of $2,640,000 (up from $2,560,000), with an SUV limit of $33,100 (up from $32,000).
  • Earned Income Tax Credit: Up to $8,496 for three or more qualifying children (up from $8,231). The release says the other categories, income thresholds and phase-outs are in a table in Rev. Proc. 2026-35.
  • Foreign earned income exclusion: $137,300 (up from $132,900).
  • Personal exemption: Remains $0.

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • These amounts apply to 2027 income. They do not change the return you file this spring for tax year 2026, so do not mix them up. Plan your year-end moves using this year's numbers.
  • With a new standard deduction and brackets, the same income can produce a different tax bill in 2027. When you set your Form W-4 withholding or your self-employed estimated payments early next year, rerun the numbers with the new amounts. Official tools such as the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator can help.
  • The release does not include brackets for head of household or married filing separately, or the full EITC table. If either applies to you, check the Rev. Proc. 2026-35 text.
  • California calculates its own tax separately. This IRS announcement gives no California amounts, so the state effect needs individual confirmation.
  • Gift and estate amounts often need to be read together with reporting rules for money sent or gifted to family abroad and for foreign assets. Every situation differs, so confirm individually.
