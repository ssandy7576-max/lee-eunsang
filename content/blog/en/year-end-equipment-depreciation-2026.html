If you plan to buy business equipment, computers, furniture or a vehicle, year-end matters. The 2025 tax law made 100% bonus depreciation permanent, and the 2026 Section 179 limit went up. To deduct it this year, though, the asset must be placed in service by December 31, and California calculates depreciation differently.

IRS Announcement

“permanent 100% additional first year depreciation deduction”IRS, IR-2026-06 · Notice 2026-11 (Jan. 14, 2026) · Read the original

Key Points

  • 100% bonus depreciation: Qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025 can be fully deducted in the first year, and the rule is permanent (IRS Notice 2026-11).
  • 2026 Section 179: Up to $2,560,000 can be expensed. The limit is reduced when total Section 179 property placed in service during 2026 exceeds $4,090,000 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32).
  • SUVs: The cost of an SUV taken into account under Section 179 is capped at $32,000.
  • Timing: Depreciation begins in the year the asset is placed in service. Ordering or paying alone does not create a deduction this year.
  • California: California does not follow bonus depreciation, and its Section 179 limit is $25,000, reduced once property placed in service exceeds $200,000 (FTB 3885A instructions).

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • Paying in December is not enough if the equipment arrives or is installed in January. That makes it next year's deduction. A year-end purchase must be ready for use by December 31.
  • For vehicles, business use generally must exceed 50% to use Section 179 or bonus depreciation, and you need a mileage log. Annual limits vary by vehicle type and weight, so confirm individually.
  • Deducting 100% is not always the right choice. If your income is low this year or you expect it to rise, spreading the deduction over several years may work better. Also keep in mind that selling the asset later can bring some of that depreciation back into taxable income.
  • Because California calculates separately, a large federal deduction may lower your state tax much less than you expect. Run federal and state numbers together before you decide.
  • Spending money you do not need just to cut taxes is a loss. Use these rules to decide when to buy equipment you need anyway.
  • Keep receipts, contracts, and delivery and installation dates. They show when the asset was placed in service.
