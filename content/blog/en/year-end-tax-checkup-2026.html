On October 23, 2026, the IRS reminded taxpayers that planning throughout the year makes the next filing season easier. As year-end approaches, it is a good time to check three things: whether your withholding is enough, whether you need to make estimated payments, and whether your tax records are organized.

IRS Announcement

“Taxpayers should plan ahead to ensure their tax filing season goes as smoothly as possible.”IRS, IR-2026-128 (Oct. 23, 2026) · Read the original

Key Points

  • Check withholding: Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator to see whether enough federal tax is being withheld from your pay.
  • After life changes: Marriage, divorce, a new child, or a job change may call for a new Form W-4 to your employer.
  • Estimated taxes: Self-employed people, small business owners, and gig workers with income that has no withholding may need to make quarterly estimated payments.
  • IRS Online Account: View balances, payments, notices, and tax records online, and request an Identity Protection PIN.
  • Organize records: Keep W-2s, 1099s, digital asset records, last year's return, bank details, IRS notices, and documents supporting credits or deductions together.
  • Beware of scams: Do not click links or open attachments in unexpected messages claiming to be from the IRS, and report suspicious emails or texts to phishing@irs.gov.

Eunsang Lee, EA: What This Means for You

  • If your income changed a lot this year, check before December. A new job, a new business, or an asset sale can leave withholding or estimated payments short. A shortfall can mean a balance due when you file and, in some cases, a penalty (confirm for your situation).
  • If you are self-employed with 1099 income, check your fourth-quarter estimated payment. Per the IRS, the payment for September 1 through December 31 is due January 15 of the following year. If you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year, you generally need to pay estimated tax, but there are exceptions, so confirm individually.
  • A new W-4 applies to your remaining paychecks, not pay already received. The closer to year-end, the less room there is to adjust, so do not wait. Ask your employer when payroll will apply the change.
  • Start gathering documents now into one place. It helps avoid delays in January. Many bank and brokerage forms arrive by the end of January, though timing varies by institution.
